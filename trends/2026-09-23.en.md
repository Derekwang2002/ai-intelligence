# AI Intelligence Trends - 2026-09-23

No new trend has enough independent evidence in this cycle.

## Trend #1: Chinese labs' open-weight agentic coding models compete at the frontier
- Status: strengthening / Medium
- First observed: 2026-08-15
- Last updated: 2026-09-23
- Evidence: vLLM 0.30 moves several serving paths for DeepSeek V4.1, GLM-5.3 and other open-weight models into a tagged stable runtime. Third-party Terminal-Bench or SWE reproduction is still missing.
- Why it matters: Self-hosted frontier coding still needs independent evaluation on public weights.
- What would confirm: Third-party Terminal-Bench or SWE runs on the weights.
- 2026-09-23 review: serving-adoption evidence strengthens, but the final public-weight reproduction gate for High confidence is still unmet. Stays strengthening / Medium.

## Trend #3: Coding agents converge into multi-agent runtimes
- Status: established / High
- First observed: 2026-08-15
- Last updated: 2026-09-23
- Evidence: RRSI adds proposal budgets, held-out tasks and pruning to automated agent-harness optimization; a long-horizon collusion study shows reward conflicts can reshape multi-agent cooperation. Both are runtime research signals, not new production adoption.
- Why it matters: The selection unit is now model, runtime, scaffold, monitoring and delegation topology.
- What would confirm: More independent production cases and convergence in orchestration interfaces.
- 2026-09-23 review: evidence on harness management and long-running coordination risk increases. Stays established / High.

## Trend #7: Agent runtimes separate broad capability from consequence control
- Status: emerging / Medium
- First observed: 2026-09-09
- Last updated: 2026-09-23
- Evidence: Across 14 models and eight domains, DUMA-Bench finds that letting users keep changing shared state raises attack success from 26.9% to 41.1%. Risk depends on the model, user and state changes permitted by the runtime.
- Why it matters: Generated commands and permitted consequences should be independently testable and auditable.
- What would confirm: One policy schema across shell, MCP and browser with public adoption telemetry.
- 2026-09-23 review: new evidence strengthens the need for two-layer acceptance tests; a unified policy schema is still missing, so the trend stays emerging / Medium.
